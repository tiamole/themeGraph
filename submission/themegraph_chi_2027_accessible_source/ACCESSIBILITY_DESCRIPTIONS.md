**Figure 4:** Four side-by-side states show refused causality with lexical fallback, prevalence-qualified conditional asymmetry with a bar-to-ring glyph, conflict-qualified temporal precedence with an interrupted line and conflict marker, and admitted temporal precedence with a solid arrow.

## Tables

**Table 1:** A representative, non-exhaustive, non-ranking comparison of eight adjacent mechanism rows across five columns: mechanism, authorized object, evidence matching, decision or consequence, and controlled downstream output. The rows cover the APT/Draco/VizLinter lineage, PCA admissibility, CAPAS, EGDA, ResearchLoop, RARE/RARE-Guard, FAVA, and ThemeGraph. ThemeGraph is distinguished by typed text-graph claims, construct/object/scope predicates, admit/typed-qualify/refuse decisions, and joint control of the statement, graph mark, requested export, and projected fallback. Closeness depends on the controlled object and consequence rather than a universal ranking.

**Table 2:** A glossary of the contract notation. It defines the number of text units; policy version, claim family, concrete claim, and governed object; permitted object signatures; evidence scope and predicates; provenance and adequacy fields; observational identifiability and predicate matching; decisions and reasons; representation, statement, and export authorization; and fallback preferences and outcomes.

**Table 3:** The fifteen Policy 2.7.0 claim types are grouped into observational, semantic-relational, causal, interpretive, human-outcome, and statistical families. Panel A identifies each claim's governed-object signature, sufficient evidence predicate, and symmetric, ordered, or unscoped matching rule. Panel B specifies required adjudication, counterevidence handling, permitted encoding, and lawful fallback. Directed arrows are available only for entailment, temporal precedence, prerequisites, and approved causal claims; several refused pair-scoped claims may fall back through the declared lexical projection.

**Table 4:** A four-case decision rule crosses matched support with counterevidence. No support and no counterevidence produces refusal for insufficiency; support alone continues through downstream gates; counterevidence alone produces refusal; and support together with counterevidence produces conflict qualification.

**Table 5:** Ten controlled Policy 2.7.0 cases report the requested claim, decision, observed evidence, controlling and complete reasons, representation, export status, and witness. Lexical association, observed temporal order, and explicit prerequisites are admitted; prevalence asymmetry and conflicting temporal annotations are qualified; unsupported temporal order, causality, low persistence, inadequate dependence treatment, and missing lineage are refused. All observed outcomes match their authored expectations.

**Table 6:** Five certificate states demonstrate representation enforcement. Admission permits a semantic statement, directed arrow, and export. Prevalence qualification permits a qualified statistic and conditional-asymmetry glyph. Conflict qualification uses a contested relation and blocks export. Refusal with fallback permits only an undirected lexical link, while refusal without fallback emits no authorized edge; that absence is not a negative semantic relation. Every state conforms to its certificate.

**Table 7:** Three adversarial fixtures are evaluated under four output-control conditions. Graph-only, warning-layer, and certificate-visible-but-non-enforcing conditions each leave 3/3 unauthorized statements, 3/3 marks, and 3/3 exports reachable, with 0/3 lawful fallbacks. ThemeGraph certificate enforcement leaves 0/3 of those unauthorized outputs reachable and preserves 1/3 authorized fallback.

**Table 8:** Seven one-at-a-time contract-field removals or corruptions expose invalid behavior. Removing object type, scope/orientation, polarity, projection provenance, fallback projection, export authorization, or required adjudication enables cross-object licensing, directional transfer, counterevidence promotion, lineage loss, silent downgrade, export leakage, or unapproved causal promotion. All seven faults are detected.

**Table 9:** A deterministic transition map covers MRPC, PAWS, SNLI, AL-CPL, and TORQUE. Construct-matched positive annotations authorize only their corresponding paraphrase, entailment, prerequisite, or temporal claims. Lexical-only evidence, neutral or negative annotations, mention order, reversed relations, and cross-construct requests produce the expected refusals through insufficiency, counterevidence, scope-mismatch, or construct-mismatch routes. Expected and observed outcomes agree throughout.

**Table 10:** A 24-configuration specification surface compares ACM text, O*NET, Python standard-library text, and synthetic data. The table reports default and union edge counts, minimum and mean persistence, default-edge mean persistence, one-to-one component overlap, and counts above and below the prespecified 0.80 persistence gate. Of 9,118 ACM union edges, 289 meet the gate; O*NET has 9 of 3,520; Python has 135 of 1,063; and synthetic data has 27 of 147.

**Supplementary policy-lint table:** Twenty-one targeted Policy 2.7.0 mutations are grouped into declaration/schema, evidence typing, causal or interpretive adjudication, fallback/projection, representation/export, and semantic-versioning families.
